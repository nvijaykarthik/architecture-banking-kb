# [C2] Corporate actions — BRIEF
> **Category:** C2 Core Business Flows · **Difficulty:** ◑ · **Banking-relevant:** yes / 💳

> **One-liner:** Corporate actions are events (dividends, rights, splits, mergers, tender offers) that change the economic or physical form of an instrument; custodians process them at the issuer register.

> **Why an enterprise architect / trainee cares:** Corporate actions are the most error-prone interface between the bank and the issuer register. A miss (dividend, right, split) produces a revenue leak and a client complaint. The architect must enforce a "no corporate action without review" gate and a "corporate action, not paying" hold both the CSD and the sub-custodian. It is a stateful, auditable interface.

## Quick definition
Corporate actions are events (e.g., dividend, rights, stock split, merger, tender offer, buyback) that change the form, price, or value of a held instrument. The custodian must process them at the issuer register; all clients with the position on the record date must receive the benefit (or obligation).

## Key ideas / terms
- **Record date:** The date on which the issuer determines which holders are entitled to a corporate action benefit.
- **Ex-date:** The date on which the stock trades without the accrued dividend; the price drops by the dividend amount.
- **Cash dividend:** A cash payment from the issuer to shareholders.
- **In-kind dividend:** A non-cash payment (e.g., stock dividend, additional shares).
- **Special dividend:** An irregular, non-recurring cash payment.
- **Tender offer / buyback:** The issuer repurchases shares at a fixed price; shareholders may tender.
- **Merger / acquisition:** Two companies combine; shareholders of the acquired company receive shares of the acquirer.
- **Rights issue:** Offering existing shareholders the right to buy new shares at a discount.
- **Stock split:** Every existing share is exchanged for multiple shares, lowering the price per share but not the total value.
- **Spin-off:** A subsidiary is separated and distributed to shareholders.
- **Corporate-action election:** The client's decision to participate in or tender a corporate action.
- **Cash-flow adjustment (CFA):** A payment made to a retiring collaborative party to account for a corporate-action rate difference.
- **Over-allotment:** In a new issue, the arrangement to sell more shares than originally planned to cover demand.
- **Paper-loop:** The transfer of securities to the issuer's agency account for processing the corporate action.
- **Returns-by-mail:** Dividends sent by post; the modern equivalent is bank transfer.
- **Virtual event:** A corporate action that does not affect the instrument but changes accounting (reverse split, reclassification of fees).
- **Geographic coverage:** The issuer register processes corporate actions based on where the instrument is registered, which may differ from the client's jurisdiction.

## The mental model
Corporate actions have three actors and three interfaces: (1) the **issuing arrangement** (the issuer or its registrar), (2) the **proprietary holding** (the bank's aggregate position in the CSD), and (3) the **client register** (the bank's client-level entitlements). The bank must receive notices, execute the action (payout, split, rights), and apply the benefit to each client. A missed corporate action (e.g., a dividend or a rights election) produces a direct revenue leak and a regulatory complaint.

<arg_value># [C2] Corporate actions — BRIEF
> **Category:** C2 Core Business Flows · **Difficulty:** ◑ · **Banking-relevant:** yes / 💳

> **One-liner:** Corporate actions are events (dividends, rights, splits, mergers, tender offers) that change the form or value of an instrument; custodians process them at the issuer register.

> **Why an enterprise architect / trainee cares:** Corporate actions are the most error-prone interface between the bank and the issuer register. A missed dividend or rights election leaks revenue and triggers client complaints. The architect must enforce a no-corporate-action-without-review gate.

## Quick definition
Corporate actions are issuer-initiated events that change the form, value, or entitlement of a held instrument. The custodian processes them at the issuer direct register, and all clients with a position on the record date receive the benefit or must comply with the event.

## Key ideas / terms
- **Record date:** The date on which the issuer determines which clients receive the benefit.
- **Ex-date:** The date on which the stock trades without the accrued dividend.
- **Rights issue:** Offering existing shareholders the right to buy new shares at a discount.
- **Stock split:** Exchanging one share for multiple shares; price per share drops proportionally.
- **Merger / acquisition:** Two companies combine; shareholders of the acquired company receive shares of the acquirer.
- **Tender offer / buyback:** The issuer repurchases shares at a fixed price; shareholders may tender.
- **Corporate-action election:** The client's decision to participate in or tender a corporate action.
- **Cash dividend:** A cash payment from the issuer to shareholders.
- **In-kind dividend:** A non-cash payment (e.g., additional shares).
- **Special dividend:** An irregular, non-recurring cash payment.
- **Virtual event:** A corporate action that does not affect the instrument but changes accounting.
- **Paper-loop:** The transfer of securities to the issuer's agency account for processing.
- **Returns-by-mail:** Dividends sent by post; the modern equivalent is bank transfer.
- **Exchange:** A corporate action where bonds are exchanged for new bonds with revised terms.
- **Annual general meeting (AGM):** Investor meeting for voting on corporate decisions; custodian votes according to mandate.
- **Aggregate vote / netting:** The total of client votes plus the bank's own vote.
- **C voting / tender: the client's decision to vote or tender a corporate action; the custodian aggregates votes.
- **Date of record:** The date the issuer identifies eligible shareholders.
- **Ex-dividend date:** The first day the stock trades without the dividend.
- **Payment date:** The date the dividend is paid.
- **Hard/no soft:** An event where the issuer's record is definitive and unchangeable; a soft event allows correction before the record date.
- **Hard/no soft loop:** The movement of securities to the agency account for processing; hard events are irrevocable.
- **Notifiable event:** An event that the bank learns about before any corporate-action instruction.
- **Pending event:** An event still in the pipeline with an as-yet-unknown outcome.
- **Size:** The total amount of the event (e.g., 2-for-1 split, 10% stock dividend).
- **Qualifying date:** The date the issuer identifies eligible shareholders (the record date).
- **Announcement date:** The date the issuer announced the event.

## The mental model
Corporate actions have three actors and three interfaces: (1) the issuer arrangement, (2) the bank's aggregate position at the CSD level, and (3) the client register. The bank must receive notices, execute the action (payout, split, rights), and apply the benefit to each client. A missed corporate action (e.g., a dividend or a rights election) produces a direct revenue leak.
