# C3 12 Compliance reporting: what feeds where, regimes, content — BRIEF

> **Category:** C3 — Regulatory & compliance · **Difficulty:** ●/◑/○/◔ · **Banking-relevant:** yes 💳

> **One-liner:** Compliance reporting is the custody architecture's consumption layer: data flows from ledger, trade system, and third-party processor into regulatory, supervisory, and internal reports across MiFID II, CSDR, LCR, and sustainability regimes.

> **Why an enterprise architect / trainee cares:** Every output report is a *produced artefact*; missing a feed or a semantic mapping means the regulator receives wrong data, which means a fine. The architecture must be feed-aware, regime-aware, and content-aware.

## Quick definition

Compliance reporting is the set of mandatory and internal reports generated by a custody or investment firm to satisfy regulators, policymakers, and internal risk committees. In the custody domain, the core regimes are **MiFID II / MiFIR (transaction reporting, holdings, revenue) + CSDR (settlement discipline, CSD liability) + SFDR / Taxonomy (ESG disclosures) + LCR /Liquidity (cash and collateral reporting)**. Each regime has its own data source, periodicity, format, and recipient.

## Key ideas / terms
- **Feed:** The source pipeline (trade system, custodian ledger, corporate-actions processor, central counterparty) that delivers raw or enriched data to the reporting engine.
- **Regime:** A regulatory domain with its own reporting obligation, schema, and recipient (e.g., MiFID II RTS 1, CSDR settlement discipline, MiFIR noise-alert).
- **Content:** The semantic mapping of raw field names to regulatory field names; e.g., `CashOpening` → MiFID II `cashOpeningBalance`.
- **Periodic cycle:** Daily, intraday, monthly, quarterly, annual, event-driven.
- **Recipient:** National Competent Authority (NCA), ECB (CPSS), ESMA, EBA, or internal CRO / ALCO.

## The mental model

Think of compliance reporting as a **hub-and-spoke data mesh**:
- **Hub:** The custody data lake / event stream that normalises all feeds into a canonical model.
- **Spoke:** Each regulatory regime is a separate API endpoint or file stream, with its own schema mapping and SLA.
- **Orchestration:** A scheduler (Airflow / Prefect / dbt) triggers the mapping, validation, and dispatch pipeline.
- The **many-to-many** relationship is key: one trade can trigger MiFID II RTS 1, MiFIR noise alert, SFDR taxonomy tagging, and CSDR settlement discipline simultaneously.

## When to use / when NOT to use
- ✅ **Use when:** designing a new reporting pipeline, onboarding a new asset class, or responding to a regulator's ad-hoc data request.
- ⚠️ **Avoid when:** assuming one schema can cover all regimes without translation; MiFID II and CSDR use different identifiers (ISIN vs. LEI, trade date vs. settlement date).

## Banking example
A custody desk at a major investment bank must file:
- **Intraday MiFID II RTS 1** for equity trades: trade date, execution venue, instrument, tax code, ISIN, LEI, and channel.
- **End-of-day holdings** for UCITS: ISIN, quantity, safekeeping balance, valuation, nominal, and description.
- **Settlement discipline (CSDR) status report:** whether trades were settled on T+0 or T+2, fail reasons, and sliding-scale charges.
- **SFDR Article 8 / 9 disclosures:** taxonomy-tagged holdings, principal adverse impact indicators (PAIs).
If the corporate-actions feed is delayed by one hour, the MiFID II holding disclosure is stale, and the NCA can reject it. The architecture must therefore treat the corporate-action feed as a **critical path** in the daily reporting SLA.

## Common confusions (don't mix these up)
- **MiFID II RTS 1** vs **Transaction reporting (MiFIR):** RTS 1 is transaction reporting; MiFIR is the *regulation* that contains the noise-alert mechanism and the reporting obligation. RTS 1 is the *standard*; MiFIR is the *legal basis*.
- **CSDR settlement discipline** vs **LCR liquidity reporting:** CSDR tracks whether trades settled; LCR tracks whether the firm has enough HQLA to survive a 30-day stress scenario. One is operational; the other is prudential.
- **SFDR Article 8 / 9** vs **Taxonomy-aligned reporting:** SFDR is about *disclosure* of environmental / social impact; Taxonomy is about *classification* of economic activities as environmentally sustainable. The same holding must be tagged for both, but the outputs go to different committees.

## Interview / recall prompt
> "Explain compliance reporting for custody in 2 minutes without notes."
- Feed normalization to a custody data hub.
- Regime-specific schema mapping (MiFID II, CSDR, SFDR).
- SLA-driven orchestration (daily, intraday, event-driven).
- Validation and audit-trail before dispatch.
- Recipient routing (NCA, ECB, ESMA, internal).
